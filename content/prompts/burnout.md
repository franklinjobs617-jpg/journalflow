Burnout doesn't announce itself clearly. It tends to creep up — the tasks that used to be energizing start feeling heavy, the things you used to care about start feeling distant, and you start moving through your days on autopilot. These 100 journal prompts for burnout are designed to help you understand what's happening, reconnect with what you actually need, and figure out what a sustainable version of your life might look like. Start slow. Even five minutes of honest writing is more useful than another hour of pushing through.

## Understanding Your Burnout

1. On a scale of 1–10, how burned out do you feel right now? What's contributing most to that number?
2. When did you first notice something was off? What were the early signs?
3. What does burnout feel like in your body? Where do you carry it?
4. What's been draining you most — work, relationships, caregiving, all of it?
5. What do you do on autopilot now that used to require your full engagement?
6. What have you stopped caring about that you used to care about?
7. When did you last feel genuinely energized by your work or daily life?
8. What are you doing just to get through the day?
9. What's the thought you have most often at the start of a workday?
10. What does a hard week look like for you right now? Describe it.
11. What have you sacrificed in the name of productivity or keeping up?
12. Write about the gap between who you are at work and who you are when you're off.
13. What expectations — from yourself or others — are most exhausting to meet?
14. What does your inner critic say to you when you're not performing at your best?
15. What are you afraid will happen if you slow down?
16. What would you do if you genuinely didn't care what anyone thought of your output?
17. What are you resenting right now? Be specific.
18. What did you used to love about your work or life that feels hard to access now?
19. What's the most draining part of your week? Can anything change about it?
20. Write about what exhaustion feels like for you — not just physical, but all the kinds.

## What You Actually Need

21. If you could take a full week with no responsibilities, what would you do?
22. What does real rest look like for you — not sleep, but actual restoration?
23. What would make your day feel more sustainable right now?
24. What's something you need that you haven't asked for?
25. What's a boundary you need to set that you keep putting off?
26. What would you stop doing tomorrow if you had permission?
27. What would you start doing if you weren't so depleted?
28. What does "enough" look like in your work life? Are you anywhere near it?
29. What kind of support would actually help — not just the support people offer?
30. What does your body need right now that you keep overriding?
31. What would it feel like to not be needed by anyone for 24 hours?
32. What's something you used to do to recharge that you've stopped doing?
33. What does joy look like in your life right now? When did you last feel it?
34. What's something small you could protect in your schedule this week — just for you?
35. Write about what a sustainable version of your current life would look like.
36. What are you saying yes to that you want to say no to?
37. What are you saying no to that you actually want in your life?
38. What would you do with your time if you weren't so tired?
39. What does your gut say about what needs to change?
40. Write about what "recovery" looks like for you — not just rest, but actual rebuilding.

## Reconnecting with Yourself

41. Who were you before this season of burnout? What's still true about that person?
42. What do you value that your current life isn't reflecting?
43. Write about something you used to be excited about. Where did that excitement go?
44. What's a part of yourself that's gone quiet that you miss?
45. What did you think your work life would feel like? What's different?
46. What would you be doing if the only criterion was that it felt meaningful?
47. What does purpose feel like for you, when you've felt it?
48. What's something you're good at that you're not using right now?
49. Write about a time in your life when you felt genuinely alive in your work or daily routine. What was different?
50. What do you actually care about — not what you're supposed to care about?
51. What's something you know about yourself that burnout has made you forget?
52. Write about what "thriving" looks like for you — specifically, in terms of daily life.
53. What's a version of your work life that would feel sustainable and meaningful?
54. What's one thing you could reclaim this month — even a small piece of something you've lost?
55. Write about what alignment feels like when you've had it. What were the conditions?

## Making Changes

56. What's one thing you could change immediately that would reduce the pressure?
57. What's one thing you could ask for that you haven't asked for yet?
58. What's a commitment you need to renegotiate with yourself or someone else?
59. Write about what it would mean to take your limits seriously — as information, not failure.
60. What's something you've been pushing through that actually needs to stop?
61. What would you do differently if you believed your wellbeing mattered as much as your output?
62. What's a belief about work, productivity, or achievement that might be making things worse?
63. Write about what it would mean to give yourself permission to rest without earning it first.
64. What's one conversation you need to have that you've been avoiding?
65. What's a small, concrete change you could make this week?
66. What would you tell a friend who was in your exact situation right now?
67. What does recovery require of you that you're resistant to?
68. Write about what "sustainable" means to you in practice, not in theory.
69. What's something you've been doing out of obligation that you could release?
70. What would it mean to build your life around what restores you instead of around what depletes you?

## Longer-Term Reflection

71. How long have you been running on empty? When did this actually start?
72. What have you been telling yourself that has kept you pushing through?
73. Write about what this burnout has cost you — not just professionally, but personally.
74. What has this experience taught you about your limits?
75. What's a pattern that burnout has exposed that you need to address?
76. Write about the relationship between how you were raised and how you relate to work and productivity.
77. What would a life without burnout look like — genuinely, in detail?
78. What do you want your relationship with work to look like in three years?
79. Write about what success means to you — not the definition you've been operating from, but the one that's actually yours.
80. What's something this experience has clarified about what you actually want?
81. What would you give up to feel less depleted? Are you willing to?
82. Write about what you've been trying to prove, and to whom.
83. What's something about how you work or live that you can see now wasn't working?
84. What would you tell your past self about how to prevent this?
85. Write about what you want your life to look like when you're on the other side of this.

## Finding Your Way Back

86. What's one small thing that gave you energy this week, even briefly?
87. Write about a moment recently when you felt like yourself — even just for a moment.
88. What's something that still matters to you, even in the middle of all this?
89. Write about someone or something that reminds you of who you are when you're not depleted.
90. What's a version of joy that feels accessible right now, even if it's small?
91. Write about something you're looking forward to, however small.
92. What's one thing you're doing right that you're not giving yourself credit for?
93. Write about what hope looks like right now — if it's there, even faintly.
94. What's something that has stayed constant and good, even in this hard season?
95. Write about a time you recovered from something hard before. What helped?
96. What does the version of yourself that has come through burnout look like?
97. What's one thing you can do today that is kind to your future self?
98. Write about what you want to build when you have more capacity — what matters most?
99. What's something about yourself that burnout hasn't been able to touch?
100. Write about what you're ready for — when you're ready.
