Shadow work is the practice of getting honest with the parts of yourself you'd rather not look at  -  the anger you've suppressed, the patterns you keep repeating, the fears that run quietly in the background of your decisions. It's not comfortable. But it's one of the most powerful things you can do for your own growth. These 100 shadow work journal prompts are designed to help you approach that territory with curiosity rather than judgment. They draw on Jungian psychology and trauma-informed practices, but they're written in accessible language  -  no jargon, no preachiness. Important note: go at your own pace. Shadow work can surface strong emotions. If anything brings up material that feels too heavy to hold alone, please reach out to a therapist or trusted person. These prompts are a starting point, not a substitute for professional support.

## Shadow Work Journal Prompts: Meeting Your Shadow

1. What's a part of yourself that you never let other people see? What would happen if they did?
2. What's an emotion you feel most ashamed of? When does it show up?
3. What qualities do you most dislike in other people? How might those qualities exist in you?
4. What are you most afraid people would think if they really knew you?
5. What do you pretend not to want because wanting it feels wrong or embarrassing?
6. Is there a part of yourself you've rejected because someone told you it was too much, too loud, too needy?
7. What behavior do you do in private that you'd never admit to in public?
8. What's a thought you've had that shocked you or made you feel like a bad person?
9. What anger are you carrying that you've never let yourself fully feel?
10. What's the most selfish thing you want? Why do you feel guilty about wanting it?
11. What do you envy in others? What does that envy tell you about what you want for yourself?
12. What's a part of your past self that you've cut off and refused to look at?
13. When do you lie to yourself, even subtly?
14. What's something you've done that you haven't forgiven yourself for?
15. What does the version of you that nobody likes look like? What triggers it to come out?
16. What are you hiding underneath the version of yourself you show the world?
17. What's a need you have that you've labeled as weakness?
18. What does your inner child most need that you're not giving yourself?
19. What trauma response have you mistaken for a personality trait?
20. What aspects of yourself have you abandoned in order to be accepted?
21. Where in your life are you performing rather than being?
22. What are the rules you live by that were never actually your rules to begin with?
23. What's the most uncomfortable truth about who you are right now?
24. What are you in denial about?
25. What part of yourself have you most aggressively tried to change or suppress?

## Shadow Work Journaling Prompts for Patterns and Wounds

26. What pattern keeps showing up in your relationships, no matter who the other person is?
27. What's your first move when you feel threatened or vulnerable?
28. When do you become someone you don't recognize? What triggers that version of you?
29. What wounds from your past are still running your present decisions?
30. How do you sabotage yourself right before things get good?
31. What's your dominant defense mechanism  -  avoidance, control, humor, minimizing, aggression?
32. What does your behavior look like when you're scared but won't admit it?
33. Write about a relationship where you kept repeating the same painful dynamic. What was the loop?
34. What do you do when you feel powerless? Is it helpful?
35. What's the story you tell about your past that might be protecting you from looking at something else?
36. What early experience taught you that you weren't safe to be fully yourself?
37. Where in your life do you abandon yourself in order to keep the peace?
38. What do you do to get love or approval that actually costs you too much?
39. What are your control behaviors  -  the things you do when you feel your environment is unstable?
40. What does your inner critic say in the moments before you're about to do something new or vulnerable?
41. What beliefs about yourself did you form before you had the capacity to question them?
42. Where do you hold back love or connection because you're waiting for some condition to be met?
43. What pain have you intellectualized rather than actually felt?
44. Write about a coping mechanism you developed as a child that isn't serving you anymore.
45. What are you afraid of finding if you look too closely at yourself?
46. Where in your life do you feel like a fraud?
47. What's a way you harm yourself in small, quiet ways  -  not physically, but through choices, self-talk, self-denial?
48. What does shame feel like in your body? What does it make you do?
49. What's the thing you're most afraid of becoming?
50. What part of your healing are you most resistant to, and why?

## Shadow Work Journal Prompts for Projections and Mirrors

51. Who in your life irritates you the most? What is it about them specifically that gets under your skin?
52. What do you consistently judge in others that you refuse to examine in yourself?
53. Write about someone you've put on a pedestal. What qualities do you see in them that you haven't claimed in yourself?
54. When you feel contempt for someone, what's underneath that contempt?
55. What do you see in others  -  positive or negative  -  that you feel disconnected from in yourself?
56. Who are you jealous of? What does their life have that yours doesn't? Is that something you actually want?
57. What do you most want to be seen for that you never feel recognized for?
58. Write about a conflict you've had where, in hindsight, you played more of a role than you admitted at the time.
59. What compliments make you uncomfortable? What is it about being seen that way that's hard to receive?
60. What criticism hits you hardest? What might it be touching that's true?
61. Who do you feel superior to, and what does that superiority protect you from feeling?
62. What in the people you love most also makes you feel the most frustrated?
63. Write about someone who reflects a version of yourself you've tried to leave behind.
64. What does the way you respond to other people's success reveal about your relationship with your own worth?
65. Write about someone who changed you  -  for better or worse  -  by being a mirror.

## Shadow Work Journaling Prompts for Integration and Healing

66. What part of your shadow, if you stopped fighting it, might actually have something to teach you?
67. What would it mean to accept yourself fully  -  including the parts you've been ashamed of?
68. What's one buried part of yourself that, if you reclaimed it, would make you more whole?
69. Write about a moment when your shadow showed up and you were actually able to observe it rather than act from it.
70. What does self-forgiveness actually look like for you  -  not as an idea, but as something you practice?
71. What would you do differently if you stopped punishing yourself?
72. What's the compassionate interpretation of one of your most difficult patterns?
73. What might the most rejected part of yourself be trying to protect you from?
74. Write about a time you integrated something uncomfortable about yourself. What changed?
75. What does it feel like to be responsible for your own patterns rather than blaming circumstances or others?
76. What would your life look like if the energy you spend suppressing parts of yourself was freed up?
77. How might one of your "flaws" actually be a distorted strength?
78. What does it feel like to sit with something uncomfortable about yourself without immediately trying to fix it?
79. Write about the relationship between your shadow and your creativity, your sexuality, or your ambition.
80. What's one step you could take toward accepting a part of yourself you've been at war with?
81. What old version of yourself deserves compassion, even if their choices were messy?
82. What wound has been asking you to look at it for years that you keep walking past?
83. Write a dialogue between your ego  -  the self you present  -  and your shadow. What does each one need from the other?
84. What do you think shadow work is really asking of you, at the deepest level?
85. What's the most loving thing you could say to the part of yourself you've hidden most carefully?
86. How might accepting your full self  -  light and shadow  -  change how you relate to others?
87. What would it mean to stop seeing parts of yourself as enemies and start seeing them as parts of the same whole?
88. Write about a moment when you were honest about something hard and it actually helped rather than hurt.
89. What part of your shadow has shown up as your greatest teacher?
90. What's the difference between being aware of your shadow and being defined by it?
91. Write about how shame has shaped you. What would you be without it?
92. What does integration look like  -  not as a concept, but in your real, daily behavior?
93. What's one thing you've learned from doing this kind of introspective work that has genuinely changed you?
94. How have your wounds made you more capable of empathy?
95. What would it mean to be a whole person rather than a curated one?
96. Write about the courage it takes to look honestly at yourself. Where does that courage come from in you?
97. What do you want your relationship with your own shadow to look like?
98. What's one thing you're finally ready to acknowledge about yourself?
99. Write a letter to the part of yourself you've been most afraid to meet. What do you want them to know?
100. What does wholeness feel like when you get glimpses of it? What brings you there?
