Journaling between therapy sessions can be one of the most powerful things you do for your mental health  -  it helps you process what came up, notice patterns between sessions, and arrive at your next appointment with more clarity. These 100 therapy journal prompts are grounded in CBT, DBT, and trauma-informed approaches, but written in plain, accessible language. They work equally well as a standalone reflective practice for anyone on a healing journey who doesn't have a therapist right now. Go at your own pace. If something brings up more than you can hold, please reach out to a professional.


## Between Sessions

1. What came up in your last therapy session that you're still thinking about?
2. What's something you said in therapy that surprised you  -  something you didn't know you thought or felt until you said it?
3. What's a pattern your therapist has pointed out that you're starting to see on your own?
4. What's something you've been avoiding bringing up in therapy? What makes it hard to say?
5. What insight from therapy have you actually put into practice this week? How did it go?
6. Write about a moment this week where you caught yourself using a coping skill. What happened?
7. What's something you want to bring to your next session that you haven't figured out how to say?
8. What's shifting in how you see yourself, even slightly, since starting therapy?
9. Write about a difficult moment this week and how you handled it compared to how you might have handled it before.
10. What's your relationship with therapy right now  -  are you finding it helpful, hard, both?
11. What's something you're resisting in therapy? What do you think the resistance is protecting?
12. Write about a time this week you noticed an old pattern starting and made a different choice.
13. What does progress look like for you right now  -  not a grand transformation, but small, real movement?
14. What's a question your therapist has asked that keeps coming back to you?
15. What are you learning about yourself through this process that you couldn't have learned any other way?

## CBT  -  Thoughts & Beliefs

16. Write about a thought you had today that made you feel worse. Is it a fact, or an interpretation?
17. What's a core belief you hold about yourself that therapy is helping you examine?
18. When something goes wrong, what's your automatic explanation? Whose fault is it? How permanent does it feel?
19. Write about a time you catastrophized. What actually happened versus what you imagined?
20. What's a thought distortion you recognize in yourself most often  -  all-or-nothing, mind reading, fortune telling?
21. Write down a negative automatic thought and then write three pieces of evidence against it.
22. What's a "should" statement you carry that is creating unnecessary guilt or pressure?
23. Write about a belief you formed in childhood that no longer serves you.
24. What does your inner critic say most consistently? Where did it learn that?
25. What would you tell a close friend who was thinking what you're thinking about yourself right now?
26. Write about a situation you interpreted negatively. What are two other possible interpretations?
27. What's something you've been telling yourself that is "all or nothing"? What's the middle ground?
28. When you're at your most self-critical, what are you really afraid of underneath the criticism?
29. Write about a thought that has controlled your behavior more than the actual situation warranted.
30. What's a belief you're in the process of rewriting? What's the new version you're working toward?

## DBT  -  Emotion Regulation

31. Name the emotion you're feeling right now. Where do you feel it in your body?
32. What triggered a strong emotional reaction this week? What was the primary emotion, and what was underneath it?
33. Write about a time you used "opposite action"  -  doing the opposite of what your emotion was pushing you to do. What happened?
34. What's a situation where you need to practice "radical acceptance" right now  -  accepting reality as it is, not as you wish it were?
35. Write about a time this week you "rode the wave" of an emotion instead of acting on it or pushing it away.
36. What does your emotional baseline feel like this week? What's affecting it?
37. What are your most reliable self-soothing strategies? When do you use them?
38. Write about a time you were able to name an emotion before it took over. What was different about that moment?
39. What does "wise mind" feel like for you  -  the place where emotion and reason meet? When do you find it?
40. Write about a relationship where you practice interpersonal effectiveness skills. How is it going?
41. What's a distress tolerance strategy you've been using? Is it working?
42. Write about a moment you checked the facts before reacting to an emotional interpretation.
43. What emotion do you have the most difficulty regulating? What does it feel like when it takes over?
44. Write about a time you asked for what you needed effectively. How did it feel?
45. What's a value you hold that helps guide you when emotions are high?

## Trauma & Healing

46. Write about something from your past that is still showing up in your present  -  in your reactions, your choices, your body.
47. What does "triggered" feel like for you, physically and emotionally?
48. Write about a coping mechanism you developed in response to something hard. Does it still serve you?
49. What does safety feel like for you? When do you experience it?
50. Write about a relationship pattern you recognize as connected to something from your past.
51. What does your nervous system do when you feel threatened? What does that look like from the outside?
52. Write about a time you felt a trauma response and were able to recognize it as such, in the moment or later.
53. What would "healing" look like for this particular wound  -  not completely gone, but carried differently?
54. Write about a moment of genuine safety  -  with a person, in a place, in yourself.
55. What do you need to feel safe enough to do the healing work?
56. Write about the relationship between your body and your trauma. Where does it live?
57. What's something you've been able to do, or feel, or say since starting to heal that you couldn't before?
58. Write about a part of yourself that got smaller or went quiet because of something that happened to you. What was that part like before?
59. What would you want to say to the person or situation that hurt you, if you could  -  not for them, but for you?
60. Write about the relationship between what happened to you and who you became. What did you carry? What did you build?

## Self-Awareness & Insight

61. What's a defense mechanism you use frequently? What is it protecting you from?
62. Write about the difference between your public self and your private self.
63. What emotions do you have the hardest time acknowledging or expressing?
64. Write about a relationship where you project  -  where you see something in someone else that might actually be about you.
65. What's something you consistently avoid? What would happen if you didn't?
66. Write about the connection between a current struggle and something from your earlier life.
67. What's a way you self-sabotage right before things go well?
68. Write about how you handle intimacy and closeness. Is it easy? Hard? Both?
69. What's your attachment style in relationships, and where do you see it playing out?
70. Write about a moment when you were able to observe yourself  -  to step back and watch your own patterns with some distance.
71. What's the emotion underneath your anger, when anger is the first thing that shows up?
72. Write about what you need when you're struggling. How well do you communicate that to others?
73. What's a way you numb yourself that you're becoming more aware of?
74. Write about the relationship between your childhood experiences and your adult fears.
75. What's something your therapist understands about you that you're still learning to understand about yourself?

## Integration & Moving Forward

76. What's one thing you've integrated  -  truly internalized  -  from your therapeutic work?
77. Write about a moment when you responded to a situation in a way that felt genuinely new.
78. What does it feel like when you access your healthy self  -  the part that isn't running on old patterns?
79. Write about a relationship that has changed because you've changed.
80. What would your life look like if you lived fully from your values rather than your fears?
81. Write about the difference between where you were when you started this work and where you are now. Even small differences count.
82. What's a story about yourself that you've been able to rewrite?
83. Write about what self-compassion has started to look like in practice  -  not the concept, but the actual experience.
84. What's something you now know about yourself that you didn't know a year ago?
85. Write about a moment of real, felt connection  -  with yourself or with someone else.
86. What does it feel like to be on your own side?
87. Write about a boundary you've set or maintained that has protected something important.
88. What part of yourself have you reclaimed that you'd let go of?
89. Write about what thriving looks like for you  -  not being cured, but genuinely living well.
90. What's a fear that has gotten smaller? What made that possible?
91. Write about what the ongoing practice of mental health looks like for you  -  what you're committed to.
92. What's something you've forgiven  -  yourself, someone else, a situation  -  that you've been carrying for too long?
93. Write about what you want your relationship with your mental health to look like in five years.
94. What are you learning about what you need to flourish?
95. Write about a moment when you felt genuine hope  -  not optimism, but real hope, grounded in something.
96. What would you tell someone who is just starting their own therapy journey?
97. What's the most important thing this work has taught you about yourself?
98. Write about who you are becoming as a result of doing this work.
99. What do you want to carry forward from this chapter of your healing?
100. If your future, integrated self could speak to the you who is still in the middle of it, what would they say?
